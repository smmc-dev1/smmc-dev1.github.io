Task relevant information extraction through saliency map and memory classifier for generalization in visual reinforcement learning

Paper Supplementary Code arXiv

Abstract

Obtaining well-generalized policies that can adapt to dynamic environmental variations remains a challenge in visual reinforcement learning (VRL). Focusing on task-relevant information while ignoring distractions is widely regarded as an effective solution to this problem. However, existing works enhance agents' information extraction through auxiliary objectives such as contrastive learning or state aggregation; these approaches primarily operate in the training process and may not filter out unseen distractions during testing. To further increase the accuracy of information extraction and improve generalization, we propose information extraction through Saliency Map and Memory Classifier (SMMC) framework. Inspired by human behavior, one can ignore task-irrelevant information in the test based on memorized task- relevant parts in the training. SMMC operates during the test and extracts information according to what the agent focused on during training. Specifically, in the training process, SMMC segments the training observations using a pretrained segmentation model, computes saliency maps to identify task-relevant parts, and then trains a binary classifier, referred to as the memory classifier, to recognize these task-relevant parts. At test time, SMMC segments test observation and uses the memory classifier to retain only task-relevant parts, effectively extracting task-relevant information. Experiments conducted on the DeepMind Control Generalization Benchmark (DMC-GB) and Meta-World across diverse tasks and test modes, results demonstrate that SMMC can extract task-relevant patches during training and test with an accuracy exceeding 98%, achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) generalization performance in test.

Agent Awareness-driven task-relevant selection mechanism

Overall, SMMC achieves over 95% accuracy in locating the "robot" part during training. Although the saliency maps only provide a coarse indication of the approximate location of important parts, when combined with SAM and agent self-awareness-driven selection mechanism, SMMC succeed in extract task-relevant information with high accuracy. In the test, the classifier is capable of selecting task-relevant information with high accuracy (exceeding 97%) as well. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the task-relevant information extraction strategy proposed in SMMC.

We conduct additional experiments on robotic manipulation tasks: Drawer-Open and Drawer-Close from MetaWorld. SMMC consistently achieves better or comparable generalization performance across all settings. Results on the DrawerWorld benchmark further indicate that depend on agent awareness-driven task-relevant object selector, SMMC provides the policy learner with clean and invariant visual inputs, leading to superior generalization under different visual domain shifts.
